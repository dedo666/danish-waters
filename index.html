Every AIS signal in Danish waters, for one year

The Danish Maritime Authority records every message its shore stations receive and publishes the archive as open data. We took one full year of it, cleaned the data, and drew what was left as light: the brighter the line, the more ships passed. Cleaning rules

  • Ships only. Only ship MMSI numbers are kept: no base stations, aids to navigation or aircraft.
  • Valid positions. Out-of-range positions (the AIS "not available" codes) and the empty "null island" position are dropped, and so is everything outside the study area, .
  • Lone spikes dropped. A single position that jumps away and back is removed. No segment faster than is drawn.
  • No line across a silence. Two positions more than apart are not joined.
  • Moving ships on the map. The density draws only segments between and : ships moored or at anchor leave no light.
  • Land left out. Traffic well inland, mostly boats towed by road with the transponder on, is removed. Busy canals such as the Kiel Canal stay.

How to read the figures

  • The map is a sum over . It has no time: the seasons are in the charts.
  • A crossing is one pass over a strait line, in either direction: two positions no more than apart on either side of it. One ship can cross many times. Ferries that sail along a line are not counted: the Fehmarn line runs east of the Rødby–Puttgarden route.
  • A vessel-day is one vessel seen on one day (UTC).
  • Speeds are the speed over ground of position reports between .
  • No small boats in the track lines. The track lines leave out pleasure craft, fishing boats and vessels of unknown type: a single small boat's route could point to its owner. These vessels stay in the year's glow and in the figures.
  • Track lines show one month at close zoom. Each line is one ship's track, simplified for the zoom level. The line tiles carry a ship type only: no ship number and no name.
  • A place is a box drawn around a harbour, a bridge or a ferry line. Its figures count vessels with a moving position (at least ) inside the box.

What AIS does not see

  • Ships without a transponder. Large ships must carry one; many small boats do not.
  • Gaps in coverage at the edge of the archive's reach, far out in the North Sea and the Baltic.
  • Transponders switched off, and wrong static data: a ship type or length can be missing or mistyped.
